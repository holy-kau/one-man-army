# One Man Army

_Kit v1. Your agents, your rules, your final say._

You did not train for a decade to chase reminders, juggle rotas and rewrite the same email three times. Yet that is where the evenings go.

Start with what you care about, not the tools. Then build agents that take the rest off your plate.

This kit gives you a small team of AI helpers that work for one person: you. A shared folder (the "2nd brain"), a short rulebook every agent reads first, a Chief of Staff who runs the day, a few specialists with one job each, and a weekly rhythm. Agents draft, research, remind and file. They do not send, pay, book or delete unless you say yes. You keep the pen.

Doctors, picture a ward. You are the consultant. The Chief of Staff is the ward sister. The specialists are specialty nurses. The reasoning agent is the medical officer who writes the proper notes. The shared folder is the case notes. Not a doctor? `analogies.md` has the same team as an army platoon, an office, a home and a sports team. Pick the world you know from the inside.

This is a clean template, not a copy of anyone's life. The fastest way in is the **interactive primer**, a single web page that asks how you use AI now, teaches the basics, and builds a copy of this kit made for you. Everything below is the manual route.

## Safety first

Safety is the first feature, not an add-on. You hold the pen: agents draft, and nothing is sent, paid, booked or deleted without your yes. Sensitive data (money, IDs, passwords, anyone's medical details) stays offline on a computer you control. Agents never click email links or open attachments. When an agent isn't sure, it stops and asks. Full rules and the off switch are in `guardrails.md`.

## Who it is for

Busy people who want admin and follow through off their plate, without handing their life to a machine.

Doctors first. The guardrails assume you handle clinical or other confidential work, and that none of it belongs here. The same kit works for anyone who wants one reporting line and clear lanes.

This is not a clinical tool, not a medical device, and not professional advice. Never put patient, client or other identifiable information in it.

## What you need

- A folder your agents can all read and write: Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, or a folder on your computer if your tools can reach it.
- A calendar your Chief of Staff can reach.
- At least one AI assistant account. **Any** of Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok Bot or Muse will do. `tools.md` helps you choose, honestly.
- Optional: email in draft only mode, and any other connections you already trust.

One tool is enough to start. Two tools give you a second opinion. The folder is the asset; the tools are replaceable.

## Setup: about 35 minutes, one sitting

**1. Make the folder (3 min).** Create a folder called `One Man Army` and copy everything in, subfolders and all. Keep files as plain `.md`. If your drive turns one into a Google Doc or Word file, turn it back. Plain text is the point: it moves between tools without breaking.

**2. Fill the placeholders (7 min).** Open `AGENTS.md`, `memory.md` and `context/me.md`. Replace `[YOUR_NAME]`, `[YOUR_TIMEZONE]` and the short "about you" lines. No passwords, patient or client details, or account numbers.

**3. Stand up two agents (12 min).** In your chosen tool, create one project or assistant per role. Start with **Chief of Staff** and the **reasoning agent**. Paste the matching card from `agents/` as its instructions (`tools.md` says where). Tell each one: read `AGENTS.md` first, then `guardrails.md`, then only the context file for the task.

**4. Connect the folder and calendar (5 min).** Give Chief of Staff the folder and the calendar. Give the reasoning agent the folder. In each tool's settings, set sending, posting, paying, booking and deleting to "ask every time". Written rule plus tool setting: two locks, not one.

**5. Run the practice task (3 min).** Ask the reasoning agent (or Learning) to answer `inbox/TASK-20261123-1500-example-weekly-plan.md`. A `RESULT-*.md` should land in `outbox/`. Then tell Chief of Staff "check the outbox". One line back means the loop is alive.

**6. Get interviewed (5 min).** Say "interview me" to the reasoning agent. It follows `skills/interview-me` and fills `context/me.md` one question at a time, so you never write about yourself from a blank page.

**7. Turn on one routine.** The 07:00 morning brief in `routines.md`. Live with it for a week before adding anything. Restraint is part of the protocol.

## How work moves

1. You (or Chief of Staff) file one task per file in `inbox/`, using `inbox/_TEMPLATE.md`.
2. The named agent does the work and writes `outbox/RESULT-<task-file-name>.md`.
3. Chief of Staff reads new outbox files and gives you one line per item, twice a day.
4. Anything irreversible is drafted and stopped until you say yes. Decisions come one at a time.

A few phrases run the whole thing:

- "Log this": save a fact to `memory-log/` now.
- "File this for [agent]": put a task in `inbox/`, with the goal, what done looks like and what is off limits.
- "Check the inbox": process open tasks now.
- "Check the outbox": report new results now.
- "Interview me about X": fill or refresh a context file.
- "Make this a skill": turn a job that worked into a routine.
- "Stop": the agent halts, says what it changed, and logs a correction.

**Fast and slow:** if it needs watching or remembering, tell Chief of Staff. If it needs doing or thinking through, tell the reasoning agent.

## First week

Do not switch on every specialist on day one. That is how you end up managing your assistants. Run the morning brief, file a few real tasks, keep `memory.md` honest. Add Scheduler, Learning, Health or Lifestyle when you have a real, repeating job for them.

## If you practise clinically

Use this kit for your own life, study, scheduling and admin. Keep clinical work on your employer's approved tools. Strip identifiers before any AI use. Read `guardrails.md` before you connect email or a shared drive that might hold patient or client files.
